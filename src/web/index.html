Why cleanup stalls

Ocean cleanup still runs on guesswork, and smaller crews pay the highest price.

Public ocean data already exists, but actionable mission guidance usually does not. That gap leaves smaller NGOs, fishing fleets, and island nations stuck with high fuel spend, uncertain search patterns, and limited access to the tools that could make cleanup more viable.

01

Raw data without helm guidance

Ocean models exist, but individual crews still have to interpret shifting currents, debris drift, and timing on their own before they can even start collecting.

02

Fuel spent on uncertainty

Boats burn time and fuel searching for debris fields, turning cleanup missions into carbon-intensive guesswork instead of efficient recovery operations.

03

Smaller fleets get left behind

The crews closest to the problem often have the least access to predictive tools, making cleanup less equitable and less scalable where it matters most.

Under the hood

The routing layer is powered by ocean carbon intelligence, not guesswork.

OceanRoute still solves a cleanup-routing problem, but the pathing is informed by a deeper environmental model. We measure ocean sink behavior, track weakening patterns, and surface route priorities from monthly climate and observation data before crews ever leave the dock.

What the program measures

These signals help determine where the ocean is behaving abnormally, where sink strength is shifting, and where cleanup routes should be prioritized.

01

Air-sea CO2 flux

The core monthly target, built from SOCAT ocean observations, NOAA atmospheric CO2, ERA5 wind, and Copernicus fields.

02

Sink coverage and mean flux

Measures how much of the mapped ocean is absorbing carbon and the average intensity of that absorption across the surface.

03

Weakening score and route priority

Derived indicators on the map layer that help surface anomalies and rank where routing attention should go first.

04

Training quality

Tracks epoch progress, validation loss, MAE, and R² so model performance stays visible and auditable during training.

What the program accomplishes

The system turns raw monthly climate inputs into an operational workflow that supports routing, monitoring, and model iteration.

  • Aligns real monthly ocean and climate inputs into training tensors that can be used consistently across the modeling pipeline.
  • Trains a ConvLSTM-style model to recognize spatiotemporal patterns that inform cleanup route decisions.
  • Powers a mission interface for map monitoring, anomaly review, point inspection, and ML operations.

Why this matters for cleanup

Better routing is not just about drawing shorter lines on a map. It comes from understanding where the ocean system is shifting, then translating that signal into practical mission guidance for crews in the field.
